# My AI Fluency Retrospective

## Written for the person I was in Week 1

---

### What I Set Out to Do

In Week 1, I was a data science student with scattered Jupyter notebooks and a fear of deployment. I knew how to build models, but I didn't know how to ship them. I wanted to learn how to turn my messy notebooks into something a real user could actually open and use.

I also wanted to learn how to work *with* AI — not just use it as a search engine, but as a thinking partner. I wanted to stop writing code alone and start building with a collaborator.

---

### What Changed

**1. I stopped fearing deployment.**

Before: I'd build a model, write a notebook, and stop. Deployment felt like "someone else's job."

Now: I deploy everything. Every project gets a Streamlit app. Every app gets a live link. Every link gets a README. I've shipped 6 apps in 10 weeks. That's more than I shipped in the 2 years before this track.

**2. I learned to judge AI output, not just generate it.**

Before: I'd ask AI to write code and copy-paste it without thinking.

Now: I use AI to generate options, but I make the final call. I ask for 3 approaches and pick the best one. I reject what doesn't serve the project. The skill isn't writing code — it's deciding which code is worth keeping.

**3. I learned to frame my work.**

Before: My portfolio was a list of project names.

Now: My portfolio tells a story. "I turn messy data into deployed AI tools that actually get used." Every project supports that claim. Every screenshot proves it. Every demo shows it.

---

### What I'd Build Next

I want to build a **real-time monitoring dashboard** for the EV Safety System. Instead of batch predictions, I want to stream sensor data live and trigger alerts in real-time.

I also want to add **explainability** — SHAP values or LIME — so operators can see exactly *why* a charger is flagged. That's the next level of trust.

---

### The Three Most Transferable Things I Learned

**1. Judgment > Generation.**

AI can generate a hundred images, a hundred functions, a hundred designs. The skill is deciding which one to keep. I learned to reject 90% of what AI generates and keep only what serves the work.

**2. Deployment is not optional.**

A model in a notebook is a prototype. A model in an app is proof. I learned that shipping is the difference between "I can" and "I did."

**3. Honesty is credibility.**

When I say "I built this with Claude and here's what I checked myself," people trust me more, not less. Transparency about AI usage shows I understand the tool, not that I'm hiding behind it.

---

### Final Thought

10 weeks ago, I had projects. Today, I have a portfolio. I have a brand. I have a story.

I have 6 deployed apps, a professional site, a complete README, and a video showing my work running live.

More importantly, I have a new way of working — one where I don't build alone, where I don't stop at "it works on my machine," and where I don't hide how I built it.

I'm ready for what comes next.
